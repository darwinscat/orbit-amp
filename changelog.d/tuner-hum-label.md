Amps: all

### Changed
- **The tuner names mains hum for what it is.** A cable humming with nobody playing used to read as a note — 50 Hz as
  G1 +35, 60 Hz as B1 -49, for as long as it hummed. Now, when the reading stands on a mains line (50 or 60 Hz, or their
  2nd to 4th harmonics, within a few cents) and nothing has been plucked, the tuner writes **HUM 50** or **HUM 60**
  where the note's name stands, and dims the needle. Nothing is hidden: the needle still shows what it hears. A string
  reads its note while it rings, however it is plucked; opened onto hum, the tuner says HUM within a second; hum that
  comes in with a thump reads as a note for a couple of seconds, and so, once it has stopped fading, may a note held by
  a sustainer or a compressor ahead of the amp.
