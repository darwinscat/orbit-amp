Amps: all

### Changed
- **The tuner names mains hum for what it is.** A cable humming with nobody playing used to read as a note — 50 Hz as
  G1 +35, 60 Hz as B1 -49, for as long as it hummed. Now, when the reading stands on a mains line (50 or 60 Hz, or their
  2nd to 4th harmonics, within a few cents) and its level has held, the tuner writes **HUM 50** or **HUM 60** where the
  note's name stands, and dims the needle. Nothing is hidden: the needle still shows what it hears, and whenever the
  tuner is not sure, it shows the note as before. A string reads its note while it rings, however it is plucked and
  even when the tuner is opened after the pluck. Opened onto steady hum, the tuner says HUM within a second; onto hum
  that swings, within a few. Hum that comes in with a thump reads as a note for a couple of seconds; hum that a hand on
  the strings takes down reads HUM again as soon as the hand is off. A note held perfectly flat by a sustainer or a
  compressor ahead of the amp, standing on a mains line, may read HUM once it has held.
